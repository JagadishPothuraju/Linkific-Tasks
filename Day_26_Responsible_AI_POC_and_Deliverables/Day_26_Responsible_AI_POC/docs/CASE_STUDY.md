# Case Study: Gender Bias in Amazon's Experimental Hiring Tool

Reuters reported on 10 October 2018 that Amazon discontinued an experimental AI recruiting tool after it was found to disadvantage women in technical-role résumé ranking. The tool was intended to score applicants' résumés and help identify candidates.

## What happened
According to Reuters, the model learned from roughly ten years of historical résumés, most submitted by men. It learned patterns that favored male applicants. The report said the system penalized some résumés containing the word “women's” and downgraded graduates of two all-women's colleges. Editing a few terms could not guarantee that other proxy signals would not reproduce the bias. Amazon ultimately discontinued the project. Reuters reported recruiters viewed recommendations but did not rely solely on the rankings.

## Impact
- Qualified women could have been ranked lower than comparable candidates.
- Historical inequality risked being repeated through automated recommendations.
- Trust in automated recruitment was weakened.
- The case showed that removing an explicit gender field does not remove proxy bias.

## Lessons
Historical data can encode unfair patterns; models can learn proxies; fairness tests must be conducted before launch and throughout operation; meaningful human review and accountability are essential.
